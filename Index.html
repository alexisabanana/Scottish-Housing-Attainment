Scottish Housing Prices & School Attainment

Exploring the relationship between school-leaver educational attainment and median house prices across Scottish Data Zones between 2014 and 2018.

Explore the dashboard ↓ View original data

01 · Overview

About the project

This project investigates whether Scottish Data Zones with higher school-leaver attainment scores also tend to have higher median house prices. It brings together two public datasets to explore patterns across geography and time.

The aim is to demonstrate practical data analytics skills, including data preparation, joining datasets, checking data quality, exploratory analysis and interactive visualisation.

Research question Is there an observable relationship between area-level educational attainment and median house prices across Scotland?

03 · Technical skills

Tools & techniques

Tableau Public Power BI Data cleaning Data integration Geographic identifiers Calculated fields Exploratory analysis Data visualisation

The work involved preparing house-price and school-attainment datasets, aligning geography codes and comparison years, creating calculated fields, investigating unusual values and developing an interactive dashboard.

Tableau was used to present the findings in an accessible format, allowing users to explore the relationship between the two measures across the selected years.

04 · Approach

Methodology

  1. Prepare the data. Filter house-price records to the selected years, median measure and relevant Data Zone geography.
  2. Align the reporting periods. Prepare the school-leaver attainment data so its comparison year can be matched to the house-price year.
  3. Match geographic records. Use the 2011 Data Zone geography codes to connect the datasets.
  4. Explore the relationship. Use a scatter plot, year-based colours and trend lines to investigate patterns in the data.
  5. Review data quality. Check unmatched records, missing values and extreme observations before interpreting the results.

Limitations and interpretation

The attainment data uses overlapping three-year reporting periods. Consequently, adjacent comparison years are not independent observations. Coverage may also vary where records cannot be matched between datasets.

Both measures describe areas rather than individual people or households. Any observed relationship is an area-level association and does not demonstrate that educational attainment causes higher house prices, or vice versa.

The results should be interpreted as exploratory analysis for 2014–2018, not as evidence of a causal relationship or a prediction of current house prices.
